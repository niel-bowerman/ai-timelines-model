STEP 1 · WHAT METR FOUND

Anthropic's models have been improving about twice as fast since October 2025

On 5 November 2026 METR published an independent assessment of how much AI is speeding up AI research at Anthropic, after eleven weeks with access to internal data. This is its main chart, redrawn. Each dot is an Anthropic model, scored on AECI, Anthropic's index of benchmark performance. METR's chart starts in 2024, so the three earlier points come from Epoch's public index, which AECI is based on and which starts in 2023.

The report in five points

  • About 2x. Scores have risen 1.9x as fast since October 2025. METR's range across methods is 1.1x to 2.9x.
  • Mostly due to AI. METR thinks a majority of the speed-up comes from Anthropic's own use of AI, and that more chips do not explain it.
  • A change of slope, not a one-time jump. A lasting slope change predicted the later models better than a single jump did.
  • Less at other labs. Across all developers the speed-up is 1.4x.
  • Much is hidden. Anthropic redacted most of the evidence METR found most informative. METR found no clear bottleneck to further speed-up, and says faster-than-exponential growth is possible.

How to read the dots

Two lines: the pace changed once, in October 2025. A line that curves away: progress was on the old straight line until 2024 and has been pulling away from it faster and faster since, which is how an explosion would look from the inside. The straight line is fixed. The slider below changes only how sharply the curve leaves it. The explosion regime follows whichever you pick.

Your read of the speed-up

As a multiple of the earlier pace. Every regime below starts from this.

The report measures the past year. It cannot say what the line does next, and that is where most of the uncertainty in any forecast now sits.

STEP 2 · WHERE IT GOES NEXT

Three ways the pace could go from here

The pace was steady until October 2025, then roughly doubled. The question is where it goes from here. The three regimes look the same today, and differ in what limits progress once AI does most of the research. Set how likely you think each one is, and how it behaves.

Software-only explosion

Each improvement makes the next arrive sooner, even on fixed chips. Progress runs away within a year or two.

Compute-bounded

AI does most of the research, so progress runs several times faster. It then levels off, because experiments still queue for chips.

One-off step

AI tools gave a single boost. Chips and headcount set the pace again, and it drifts back towards the old trend.

SAR is a superhuman AI researcher. It has two heights: if there is no explosion, the likeliest reason is that research taste proved hard to automate, so it needs more progress. The heights are judgement calls, and nothing measured anchors them.
